\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we presented a large-scale empirical evaluation of \texttt{GPT-4o} (accessed via the cloud-based OpenAI Responses API) for joint BDD Gherkin scenario and Behave Python step definition generation across $N=500$ requirements.

Our empirical findings directly answer our three Research Questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Semantic Similarity)}: Few-Shot prompting achieves the highest mean SBERT Cosine Similarity of $0.8218$, significantly outperforming Zero-Shot ($0.8088$, $p < 0.0001, d=0.291$) and Chain-of-Thought prompting ($0.8168$, $p < 0.0001, d=0.617$).
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Executable Syntax Validity)}: Few-Shot and Chain-of-Thought prompting produce the highest executable AST syntax validity ($100.0\%$, 500/500), while Zero-Shot achieves $99.8\%$ (499/500), with all techniques vastly exceeding the $85\%$ benchmark ($p < 0.0001$).
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Comparative Effectiveness)}: Statistically significant differences exist among all three prompting techniques ($p < 0.0001$), establishing Few-Shot prompting as the most effective and token-efficient strategy for automated BDD workflows.
\end{itemize}

In summary, this study demonstrates that Few-Shot prompting with GPT-4o provides optimal semantic precision, structural completeness, and syntactic validity for enterprise BDD test automation.

Future work will focus on integrating automated HTML DOM locator discovery tools to populate the generated \texttt{pass} function bodies, evaluating additional foundation models (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet), and testing generalization across multi-domain software repositories.
